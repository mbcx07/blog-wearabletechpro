The Smartwatch Evolution: A Deep Dive into Apple, Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit's Health Arsenal

2026-06-01 · Smartwatches · Tags: wearabletechpro, smartwatches, The

The smartwatch has evolved far beyond a simple notification mirror on your wrist. In 2024, these devices have become sophisticated health command centers, capable of tracking everything from your heart's electrical activity to your blood oxygen saturation, and even predicting potential health issues before they become critical. As a wearable tech reviewer, I've spent countless hours testing the latest offerings from the industry's big four—Apple, Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit—alongside the emerging category of AR glasses that promise to overlay health data onto your visual field. The question is no longer which watch looks best, but which one truly understands your body. In this comprehensive comparison, I'll break down the strengths, weaknesses, and unique health features of each major player, helping you decide which wearable deserves a permanent spot on your wrist—and whether AR glasses are ready to join the party.

The Health Monitoring Landscape: What's New in 2024?

This year has seen a remarkable leap in sensor technology and algorithmic analysis. The once-basic step counter has given way to a suite of biometric sensors that continuously sample your physiology. Key trends include the expansion of temperature sensing for cycle tracking and fever detection, the refinement of sleep staging with AI-driven insights, and the integration of ECG and atrial fibrillation (AFib) detection across more price points. Additionally, we're seeing a push toward 'passive health'—the ability to collect data without the user actively engaging with the watch. For instance, the latest Apple Watch Series 9 and Ultra 2 use a new optical heart sensor that can capture high-resolution waveforms even during intense activity. Samsung's Galaxy Watch 6 series counters with advanced BioActive sensor that measures heart rate, blood pressure (with calibration), and body composition. Garmin, known for its athletic focus, now offers HRV status and training readiness scores that rival dedicated sports equipment. Fitbit, now under Google's umbrella, has integrated deeper with the Pixel ecosystem while retaining its user-friendly approach to health metrics. The real differentiator, however, lies in how each company interprets the data and translates it into actionable advice.

Apple Watch: The Gold Standard for Health Integration?

Apple continues to dominate the premium smartwatch market, and for good reason. The Apple Watch Series 9 and the rugged Ultra 2 are not just accessories; they are medical-grade monitoring tools. The ECG app is FDA-cleared, and the watch can detect irregular heart rhythms, alerting users to potential AFib. The addition of temperature sensing for retrospective ovulation estimates and the ability to track sleep stages with remarkable accuracy make it a comprehensive health companion. What sets Apple apart is its seamless integration with the Health app and third-party research studies. For example, the watch can share data with your physician through the Health Records feature, and its fall detection and crash detection have proven life-saving in real-world scenarios. However, the Apple Watch is not without its limitations. Battery life remains a sore point—you'll be charging it daily, which can disrupt continuous sleep tracking if you don't plan around it. Moreover, the watch is tightly coupled with the iPhone; Android users need not apply. For those deeply embedded in the Apple ecosystem, the Series 9 offers the most polished health experience, but it's not the only game in town.

Samsung Galaxy Watch: The Android Powerhouse with a Health Twist

Samsung's Galaxy Watch 6 and the new Galaxy Watch 6 Classic have made significant strides in health monitoring, particularly for Android users who want an alternative to Apple. The BioActive sensor is a marvel of miniaturization, packing optical heart rate, electrical heart signal, and bioelectrical impedance analysis into a single chip. This allows for body composition measurements—body fat percentage, skeletal muscle mass, and basal metabolic rate—that were previously only available on dedicated scales. The watch also supports blood pressure monitoring, but it requires periodic calibration with a traditional cuff, which is a minor inconvenience. Samsung's sleep coaching feature is genuinely useful, offering personalized programs based on your sleep patterns. The big advantage here is compatibility: the Galaxy Watch works with any Android phone (and even iOS with limited features), making it the go-to choice for non-Apple users. However, the health app experience is not as refined as Apple's Health, and some metrics like HRV are buried under multiple menus. Still, for the price and features, the Galaxy Watch 6 is a formidable contender.

Garmin: The Athlete's Choice for Advanced Metrics

When it comes to serious fitness and outdoor activities, Garmin is the undisputed champion. The Forerunner and Fenix series, along with the Venu for everyday wear, offer an astonishing array of metrics that go far beyond what Apple or Samsung provide. Garmin's proprietary training load, recovery time, and VO2 max estimates are based on decades of sports science research. The new HRV status feature, which tracks your heart rate variability overnight, gives you a clear picture of your readiness to train. For runners, the built-in GPS is exceptionally accurate, and the ability to create and follow complex workouts directly on the watch is a game-changer. Garmin also excels in battery life—the Fenix 7 can last up to 18 days in smartwatch mode and 57 hours in GPS mode, making it ideal for multi-day expeditions. The downside? Garmin's smartwatch features are basic compared to Apple or Samsung. You won't get a robust app store or advanced notification management. The interface is utilitarian, and the touchscreen on some models is less responsive. But if your primary goal is to optimize your athletic performance and monitor your body's response to stress, Garmin is the gold standard.

Fitbit: The Accessible Health Tracker That Keeps Evolving

Fitbit, now a part of Google, has reinvented itself as a health-first brand that balances accessibility with powerful insights. The Sense 2 and Versa 4 are the flagship models, and they excel in making health data understandable for the average user. The Sense 2 introduces a continuous electrodermal activity (EDA) sensor that detects stress responses, prompting you to log your mood and practice mindfulness. The daily readiness score, which combines activity, sleep, and heart rate variability, is presented in a simple, color-coded format that even a novice can interpret. Fitbit's sleep tracking is among the most accurate in the industry, with a Sleep Score that breaks down your sleep stages and provides actionable tips. The integration with Google Fit and the ability to sync with the Fitbit app on both Android and iOS is a plus. However, Fitbit has faced criticism for moving some advanced features behind the Premium subscription paywall. For instance, the Daily Readiness Score and detailed sleep analytics require a $9.99/month subscription. That said, the hardware is affordable, and the battery life is respectable—around six days. For someone who wants a no-nonsense health tracker without the complexity of a full smartwatch, Fitbit remains a top choice.

Beyond the Wrist: AR Glasses and the Future of Wearable Health

While smartwatches dominate the conversation, AR glasses are emerging as a complementary health wearable that could redefine how we interact with our biometric data. Imagine walking down the street and seeing your heart rate and pace displayed in your peripheral vision without glancing at your wrist. Companies like Xreal (formerly Nreal) and even Apple's rumored AR glasses are exploring this space. Currently, most AR glasses are tethered to a smartphone or a smartwatch for data, acting as a heads-up display. For example, the Xreal Air 2 can project notifications and fitness metrics from a connected Garmin watch, allowing you to keep your eyes forward during a run. The potential for health is enormous: real-time feedback on form during strength training, visual cues for breathing exercises, and even augmented reality guided meditation. However, the technology is still in its infancy. Battery life is short, the field of view is limited, and the glasses can be heavy for prolonged wear. Moreover, the health-specific applications are sparse—most AR glasses are designed for entertainment and productivity. As a reviewer, I see AR glasses as a complementary device rather than a replacement for your smartwatch. In the next few years, expect to see tighter integration, with AR glasses pulling data from your watch to create an immersive health dashboard. But for now, your wrist remains the primary hub for health monitoring.

Making the Choice: Which Wearable Fits Your Lifestyle?

With so many options, the decision ultimately comes down to your priorities. If you're an iPhone user who values a seamless, all-in-one health experience with medical-grade features, the Apple Watch Series 9 is the obvious pick. If you're an Android user who wants robust health tracking and a stylish design, the Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 offers the best balance. For athletes and outdoor enthusiasts who need advanced metrics and exceptional battery life, Garmin is unmatched. And if you're new to wearables or prefer a simpler, more affordable device that still delivers meaningful health insights, Fitbit is your gateway. But don't overlook the ecosystem factor—the apps and integrations that come with each brand. For instance, Garmin's Connect app is a treasure trove of data, but it can be overwhelming. Fitbit's app is more user-friendly but may require a subscription. Apple's Health app is the most comprehensive, but it's only useful if you're all-in on Apple. My advice: write down your top three health goals—whether it's improving sleep, training for a marathon, or managing stress—and then match the device that excels in those areas. Remember, the best wearable is the one you'll actually wear every day.

Conclusion

The smartwatch landscape in 2024 is richer and more health-focused than ever. Apple, Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit each bring unique strengths to the table, and the competition is driving rapid innovation. As we look ahead, the integration of AR glasses will likely add a new dimension to how we perceive and act on our health data. But for now, the wrist remains the epicenter of wearable health. Whether you choose the polished Apple Watch, the versatile Galaxy Watch, the athletic Garmin, or the accessible Fitbit, you're investing in a tool that can genuinely improve your well-being. The key is to choose wisely based on your personal needs, not just the latest specs. As a wearable tech reviewer, I've seen how the right device can transform someone's relationship with their health. So take your time, do your research, and let your body be the guide. Your next smartwatch isn't just a gadget—it's a partner in your health journey.
