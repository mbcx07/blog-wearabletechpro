The Health-First Smartwatch: Apple, Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit in the Age of AR Glasses

2026-08-01 · Smartwatches · Tags: The, wearabletechpro, smartwatches

For years, smartwatches were little more than wrist-mounted notification centers—convenient for checking messages without pulling out a phone, but hardly essential. That narrative has shifted dramatically. Today’s flagship smartwatches are sophisticated health monitors, capable of tracking everything from heart rhythm to blood oxygen saturation, and they’re increasingly becoming the centerpiece of a connected wellness ecosystem. As we look toward the horizon, augmented reality (AR) glasses are poised to complement these wrist-worn devices, offering a new dimension of health data visualization. In this comprehensive guide, we’ll explore how Apple, Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit are competing in this health-first era, and what role AR glasses will play in the wearable tech landscape.

The Health Monitoring Revolution

The transformation of smartwatches into medical-grade health tools has been nothing short of remarkable. The Apple Watch Series 9 and Ultra 2, for instance, feature an ECG app that can detect atrial fibrillation, a blood oxygen sensor, and a temperature sensor for cycle tracking. Samsung’s Galaxy Watch 6 series offers similar capabilities, including body composition analysis and advanced sleep coaching. Garmin, long known for its fitness-first approach, has integrated pulse oximetry, stress tracking, and even a fall detection feature that can share your location with emergency contacts. Fitbit, now under Google’s umbrella, has doubled down on health with the Sense 2, which includes an electrodermal activity (EDA) sensor for stress management and a continuous heart rate monitor.

What sets these devices apart is not just the hardware but the software algorithms that interpret raw sensor data. Apple’s Health app aggregates data from multiple sources, while Samsung’s Samsung Health offers a holistic view of your daily activity, sleep, and stress. Garmin’s Connect platform is a favorite among athletes for its detailed training metrics, and Fitbit’s app provides actionable insights with a user-friendly interface. The competition is fierce, and each brand has its strengths, but the common thread is a commitment to turning your wrist into a 24/7 health companion.

Fitness Trackers vs. Smartwatches: Where Do They Stand?

While smartwatches have absorbed many fitness tracking features, dedicated fitness trackers still hold a place in the market. Devices like the Fitbit Charge 6 and Garmin Vivosmart 5 offer longer battery life and a more streamlined form factor, making them ideal for those who prioritize simplicity and extended wear. However, the line between trackers and smartwatches is blurring. The Charge 6 now includes Google Maps integration and YouTube Music controls, while the Vivosmart 5 offers advanced sleep tracking and stress monitoring. For most users, a full-fledged smartwatch like the Apple Watch or Galaxy Watch provides a more comprehensive experience, but if you’re a minimalist who wants basic activity tracking without the distractions of a smartwatch, a dedicated tracker remains a viable option.

That said, the health monitoring capabilities of modern smartwatches have made them the default choice for many. The ability to take an ECG, measure blood oxygen, and track irregular heart rhythms is a significant advantage over basic trackers. Moreover, smartwatches now offer fall detection, crash detection, and even medication reminders, making them indispensable for older adults and those with chronic conditions. As the technology matures, we can expect even more advanced sensors, such as non-invasive glucose monitoring, to appear in both trackers and smartwatches.

The AR Glasses Factor: A New Dimension of Health Data

While smartwatches have become adept at collecting health data, the way we interact with that data is about to change. Augmented reality glasses, such as the Apple Vision Pro and Meta’s Ray-Ban Stories, are beginning to offer heads-up displays that can overlay health metrics directly into your field of view. Imagine going for a run and seeing your heart rate, pace, and distance floating in front of your eyes without ever glancing at your wrist. That’s the promise of AR glasses in the health space.

But AR glasses are not just about convenience; they have the potential to enhance health monitoring in profound ways. For example, AR glasses could display real-time glucose levels for diabetics, or provide visual cues for guided meditation and breathing exercises. They could also integrate with smartwatch sensors to create a more immersive health dashboard. While the technology is still in its infancy, several companies are exploring these possibilities. Garmin has already experimented with AR displays in its cycling headsets, and Apple’s visionOS includes health-related APIs that could be leveraged by third-party developers.

For now, AR glasses are more of a complement than a replacement for smartwatches. They lack the battery life and sensor suite of a wrist-worn device, and they’re not yet comfortable enough for all-day wear. However, as the hardware improves and the software ecosystem matures, we can expect AR glasses to become an integral part of the wearable health ecosystem, working in tandem with your smartwatch to provide a richer, more intuitive health experience.

Deep Dive: Apple Watch vs. Samsung Galaxy Watch vs. Garmin Fenix vs. Fitbit Sense

Choosing the right smartwatch for your health needs can be overwhelming, so let’s break down the key differences among the top contenders.

Apple Watch Series 9 and Ultra 2 are the gold standard for iPhone users. They offer the most comprehensive health suite, including ECG, blood oxygen, temperature sensing, and a new U2 ultra-wideband chip for precise location tracking. The watchOS ecosystem is mature, with thousands of health and fitness apps. Battery life is a weak point—typically 18 hours on a single charge—but the fast charging helps. The Ultra 2 extends that to 36 hours and adds a more rugged design, making it ideal for outdoor enthusiasts.

Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 and Watch 6 Classic are the best Android alternatives. They run on Wear OS, which now integrates with Google Fit and Samsung Health. The Galaxy Watch 6 features a BioActive sensor that measures heart rate, blood oxygen, and body composition. It also offers advanced sleep coaching and a personalized heart rate zone algorithm. Battery life is around 40 hours, and the rotating bezel on the Classic model is a beloved feature for navigation. However, some health features, like blood pressure monitoring, are only available in select regions and require calibration with a traditional cuff.

Garmin Fenix 7 and Epix Pro are the ultimate fitness and adventure watches. They boast multi-band GPS, a solar charging option, and an incredible battery life of up to 18 days in smartwatch mode. Garmin’s health tracking is second to none, with metrics like training readiness, recovery time, and VO2 max. The Fenix series also includes a flashlight, barometer, and altimeter, making it a favorite among hikers and trail runners. The downside is that Garmin’s smart features are more limited—you can receive notifications, but there’s no app store to speak of. If you’re a serious athlete, Garmin is the way to go.

Fitbit Sense 2 is the health-focused option for those who want a balance of features and affordability. It includes an EDA sensor for stress management, a skin temperature sensor, and a heart rate monitor. Fitbit’s sleep tracking is among the best, with a daily readiness score that tells you when to push harder or rest. The Sense 2 also offers a six-day battery life, which is impressive. However, it lacks the advanced features of the Apple Watch or Galaxy Watch, such as an ECG (though the original Sense had it, the Sense 2 dropped it). Fitbit’s integration with Google is still evolving, but the app remains intuitive and user-friendly.

Choosing the Right Wearable for Your Health Goals

Your choice of smartwatch should align with your specific health and fitness objectives. If you’re an iPhone user who wants the most comprehensive health monitoring and a seamless ecosystem, the Apple Watch is the obvious pick. For Android users, the Galaxy Watch offers a comparable experience with a few unique features like body composition analysis. If you’re a dedicated athlete who trains for marathons or triathlons, Garmin’s advanced metrics and long battery life are unmatched. And if you’re looking for a more affordable, stress-focused tracker with excellent sleep insights, the Fitbit Sense 2 is a solid choice.

But don’t forget about the AR glasses factor. If you’re an early adopter who wants to be at the forefront of wearable tech, consider investing in a smartwatch that can integrate with future AR glasses. Apple’s ecosystem is likely to be the most tightly integrated, but Samsung and Google are also working on AR projects. For now, the smartwatch remains the primary health device, and AR glasses are an exciting add-on that will only grow in importance.

The Future of Wearable Health

Looking ahead, the convergence of smartwatches, fitness trackers, and AR glasses will redefine how we monitor and manage our health. We’re already seeing the first steps toward non-invasive glucose monitoring, with companies like Apple and Samsung investing heavily in this technology. The next generation of wearables will likely include blood pressure monitoring without a cuff, hydration tracking, and even mental health assessments based on physiological signals. AR glasses will provide a visual layer to this data, making it easier to understand and act upon.

Moreover, the integration of AI and machine learning will enable wearables to offer personalized health recommendations, predicting potential issues before they become serious. Imagine your smartwatch detecting an irregular heart rhythm and immediately alerting your doctor, or your AR glasses guiding you through a breathing exercise when your stress levels spike. This is not science fiction; it’s the trajectory we’re on.

Conclusion

The smartwatch has evolved from a novelty to a necessity, and the competition among Apple, Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit has only accelerated the pace of innovation. Each brand brings its own strengths to the table, and the best choice depends on your individual needs and preferences. As AR glasses begin to enter the mainstream, they will complement these wrist-worn devices, offering a new way to visualize and interact with your health data. The future of wearable health is bright, and there’s never been a better time to invest in a device that puts your well-being first. Whether you’re a fitness enthusiast, a tech lover, or someone managing a chronic condition, the right smartwatch can be a powerful ally in your health journey.
